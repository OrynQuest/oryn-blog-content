Search "things to do in Southern California with kids" and you get beaches, parks and big day trips. Those are great. But most family weeks are built from smaller things: a Tuesday art class, a Saturday practice, a music lesson after school. This guide is about those weekly activities. What kinds there are, what fits each age, how to choose, and how to make it all fit into a real week.

## The main kinds of weekly activities

Most kids' classes fall into a handful of groups:

- **Arts and crafts.** Drawing, painting, clay and making things. Good for kids who like quiet focus and taking something home.
- **Sports and fitness.** Team sports, swimming, martial arts, gymnastics and movement classes. Good for kids with energy to burn.
- **Music and dance.** Instrument lessons, singing, ballet, hip hop. These build steadily week by week.
- **STEM and science.** Building, coding, robotics and hands-on experiments. Good for kids who take things apart to see how they work.
- **Cooking and baking.** Measuring, mixing and tasting. A fun way to practice reading and math without calling it that.
- **Language and reading.** A second language, reading help or writing clubs.
- **Social skills.** Small, guided groups where kids practice sharing, turn-taking and making friends.
- **Camps and outdoors.** Nature programs and day camps, often during school breaks.

You don't need one from every group. Pick from what your child already talks about.

## What fits at each age

### Ages 3 to 5: short, playful and close to home

Little kids do best with short classes full of movement, songs and hands-on play. Look for small groups, a teacher who gets down to their level, and a class length that ends before the wiggles take over. A parent-and-child class can help a shy child settle in. At this age, the goal is fun, not skill.

### Ages 6 to 9: trying lots of things

This is a great age to sample. Kids can follow simple steps and start to see their own progress. Many families try a sport, an art or music class, and something new, like science or cooking, across a year. If a class doesn't click after a fair try, it is fine to move on.

### Ages 10 to 13: going deeper

Older kids often find one or two things they really care about. They may want more time on those and less on the rest. Let them lead. Ask what they enjoy most, and whether they want a bigger challenge or just time with friends.

## How to choose a class

You don't need to be an expert to judge a class. A few simple checks go a long way:

1. **Start with your child.** What do they play when no one is telling them what to do? That is a strong clue.
2. **Check the age range.** A class built for your child's age moves at the right speed.
3. **Look at the drive.** Southern California traffic is real. A class 10 minutes away at 3 pm can take much longer at 5 pm. Try the drive at the time you would really leave.
4. **Read the description and reviews.** Look for what kids actually do in a session, and what other parents say.
5. **Try one session first.** Watch how your child walks out. Smiling and chatting is a good sign.

For a longer checklist, read [how to compare children's activity classes before you book](/blog/how-to-compare-children-s-activity-classes-before-you-book).

## How to fit activities into your week

A good week has room to breathe. Some simple ideas:

- **One or two activities is plenty** for most kids, especially when school is busy.
- **Keep some afternoons free.** Kids need time to rest, play and be bored.
- **Group by place.** If two classes are near each other, siblings can go at the same time, or one after the other.
- **Watch the after-school slot.** Tired, hungry kids don't learn much. A snack in the car helps.
- **Think about the season.** Southern California weather means outdoor classes run much of the year, but afternoons can be hot. Shade, water and sunscreen matter. Indoor classes are a good swap on very hot days.
- **Plan for school breaks.** A short camp or a few drop-in sessions can fill a long break without a big commitment.

If something stops being fun for the whole family, it is fine to cut back.

## FAQ

### What are good weekly activities for young kids?

Short, playful classes work best for ages 3 to 5: movement, music, art and simple hands-on play. Look for small groups and a class length that suits your child's attention span.

### How many activities should my child do each week?

For most kids, one or two is plenty. Leave time for rest, homework and free play, and cut back if your child seems tired or stressed.
