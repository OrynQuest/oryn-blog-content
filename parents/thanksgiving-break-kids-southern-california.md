> **Key takeaways**
> - Keep a light routine. Steady sleep and meal times make the whole week easier.
> - Plan one simple thing a day, not five. Leave room for rest and boredom.
> - Mix family time, time outdoors, and maybe a drop-in class or short camp. Keep the last day quiet.

Many schools in Southern California give kids a few days off around Thanksgiving, and some give the whole week. It sounds relaxing. But a break with no shape can turn into late nights, too much screen time and cranky kids by Wednesday. Here is a simple, calm way to plan the week, so everyone gets rest and still has fun.

## Start with a light routine

Kids don't need a school-day schedule on a break. But a few steady anchors help a lot:

- **Wake-up and bed times.** Let them slide a little, not a lot. Big shifts make the first school morning hard.
- **Meals and snacks.** Regular times keep moods steady, especially on busy cooking days.
- **One quiet hour a day.** Reading, drawing, puzzles or a nap. It gives everyone a reset, including you.
- **Some screen limits.** Agree on them at the start of the week, so you aren't arguing every afternoon.

Write the plan on paper and put it on the fridge. Kids like knowing what comes next.

## A simple week shape

Every family's break looks different. Your days off may be shorter or longer. Here is one easy shape to adapt:

| Day | Idea |
|---|---|
| **First day off** | Sleep in a little. A slow morning, then time outside. |
| **Next day** | An activity: a drop-in class, a short camp day or a craft project at home. |
| **Day before the holiday** | Kids help get ready. Simple jobs, music on, no rush. |
| **The holiday** | Family time. Keep expectations low and plates simple for little ones. |
| **Day after** | Rest. Leftovers, a walk, a movie or a board game. |
| **Weekend** | Something outdoors, then a quiet evening. |
| **Last day** | Back toward school rhythm: early dinner, bags packed, early bed. |

Notice that most days have one main thing. That is on purpose.

## Make room for real rest

Kids are often tired by late November. A break is a chance to catch up on sleep and slow down. Rest doesn't have to mean lying on the couch. It can be building with blocks or cardboard, drawing, reading together, or simple, unplanned play in the yard.

A little boredom is fine. It is often where kids find their own ideas.

## Family time, the easy way

The holiday itself can be busy. A few ideas to keep it warm and calm:

- **Give kids a real job.** Setting the table, washing vegetables, folding napkins or writing name cards. Kids enjoy feeling useful.
- **Cook something small together.** Mixing a simple dough, decorating cookies or making a fruit plate. Let them measure and stir.
- **Make a thankful list.** Each person adds one thing a day. Read it out at dinner.
- **Plan a quiet corner.** A spot with books and paper where an overwhelmed child can take a break from a full house.
- **Keep bedtime close to normal.** Even on the big day, a calm wind-down helps.

## Get outside

Late November is often mild in much of Southern California, which makes the break a good time to be outdoors. Try:

- A walk around the neighborhood or a local park
- A bike or scooter ride, or a picnic lunch
- A trip to the beach to walk and explore

It gets dark early this time of year, so plan outdoor time for the morning or early afternoon. Some days can be windy or dry. Check the weather, bring water and sunscreen, and have an indoor backup.

## Maybe a drop-in class or short camp

A break doesn't need a full schedule. But one or two structured activities can help, especially for kids who miss their friends or need to move. Some local vendors offer drop-in classes or short camps during school breaks. Think about:

- **Drop-ins** for a single session. A good way to try something new, like art, cooking or a sport, without a long commitment.
- **A short camp** for a day or two. Useful if parents are working part of the week.
- **Something your child already loves.** A familiar activity can feel like a treat on a day off.

Keep it to one or two. The rest of the week is for rest. For low-cost ideas to fill the other days, see [weekend activities for kids on any budget](/blog/weekend-activities-for-kids-on-any-budget-2026-parent-s).

## Getting ready for school again

The last day of the break sets up the first day back. Move bedtime back toward the school-night time, pack bags and lay out clothes, and talk about what is coming up at school that week. A calm last evening makes the first school morning much smoother.

## FAQ

### How do I keep kids busy over Thanksgiving break without overplanning?

Plan one main thing a day, like a walk, a craft or a drop-in class. Keep the rest open for rest, play and family time.

### Should kids keep their bedtime over the break?

Let it slide a little, not a lot. Move it back toward the school-night time a day or two before school starts.
