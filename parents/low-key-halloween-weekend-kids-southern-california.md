Halloween falls on a Saturday this year. That means no rushing home from school to change, and no school the next morning. It also means a whole weekend of sugar, late nights and big feelings, plus the clocks going back an hour early on Sunday, November 1.

You don't need a packed schedule to make it special. Here is a low-key plan for the whole weekend. For more weekend ideas that work any time of year, see our [weekend activities guide](/blog/weekend-activities-for-kids-on-any-budget-2026-parent-s).

## Costumes that work on a warm night

Late October in Southern California can still feel like summer, especially inland. Then it can cool off fast once the sun goes down. Plan the costume for both.

- **Start with light layers.** A cotton T-shirt and leggings under the costume work better than a heavy, all-in-one suit. Bring a thin sweater for later.
- **Skip full masks.** They get hot and can block what a child sees. Face paint, a hat or a headband with ears is cooler and easier.
- **Check the length.** Capes, gowns and tails should stop above the ankle so nobody trips on a curb.
- **Wear real shoes.** Pick comfortable sneakers your child already wears, not new costume shoes.
- **Do a test walk.** A few minutes in the full outfit earlier in the week finds the itchy tag in time.
- **Pack water.** A small bottle helps on a warm evening.

## Trick-or-treat safety basics

These are general habits that keep the evening easy.

**Be easy to see.** It gets dark early in the evening, and costumes are often dark too. Add reflective tape to a bag or the back of a costume. Give each child a small flashlight or a glow stick to carry.

**Stick together.** Young children should go with a grown-up. Agree on a meeting spot before you start, in case anyone gets separated. Walk on sidewalks, cross at corners, and remind kids not to dart between parked cars. Older kids out with friends should stay in a group and check in at a set time.

**Check treats at home.** Make a rule that nothing gets eaten until you are home and a grown-up has looked through the bag. Set aside anything unwrapped, opened or homemade by someone you don't know. For very young children, pull out small, hard candy that is easy to choke on.

**Plan for allergies.** If your child has a food allergy, read the labels together at home and follow the plan you already use. Bring a few safe treats from home so your child can swap. If you are handing out treats yourself, a bowl of non-food items, like stickers, pencils or small toys, lets every child take something.

## A sensory-friendly Halloween

For some children, Halloween is a lot. Doorbells, masks, crowds, flashing decorations and late bedtimes can all add up. A few changes can help:

- **Preview it in daylight.** Walk the route earlier in the day so the houses and decorations are already familiar.
- **Keep it short.** A few houses on your own street can be plenty. Stop while it is still fun.
- **Go early.** Trick-or-treat right at dusk, before the busiest part of the evening.
- **Choose a comfortable costume.** Soft fabric, no mask, nothing tight. Regular clothes with a fun hat count too.
- **Bring comfort items.** Headphones for noise, a favorite toy, a snack.
- **Agree on a signal.** Give your child a word or gesture that means "I'm done," and promise that leaving early is fine.
- **Offer another job.** Some kids enjoy handing out treats at the door more than going out. That still counts as Halloween.

## A quiet daytime plan for Saturday

A slow morning gives everyone energy for the evening.

- **Sleep in if you can,** and have a filling breakfast.
- **Decorate a pumpkin.** Older kids can scoop and carve with a grown-up handling the knife. Little ones can paint or use stickers instead.
- **Read a not-too-scary story** together, or build a reading fort.
- **Have a real lunch and some downtime** before the evening. A rest, even without a nap, helps a lot.

## A crafts and baking afternoon

**Simple crafts:**

- Paper bats or ghosts to hang in a window.
- Paper-plate masks to wear around the house.
- Cardboard tube "mummies" wrapped in white paper strips.
- A treat bag your child decorates with markers and stickers.

**Easy baking:**

- Roast the pumpkin seeds from your carving.
- Decorate plain cookies with frosting faces.
- Make "mummy" pizzas with strips of cheese and two olive eyes.

Give each child one job they own, like stirring, sprinkling or placing the eyes. For more everyday ideas, see our list of [kids activities near you](/blog/kids-activities-near-me-12-ideas-southern-california-parents).

## Back to routine on Sunday and Monday

The clocks go back an hour early on Sunday, November 1. That extra hour is a gift after a late night. Use it to rest, not to fill.

- **Make a candy plan.** Sort the bag together, let your child pick a few favorites, and put the rest away for later.
- **Keep Sunday slow.** A late breakfast, a walk outside and a quiet afternoon at home.
- **Shift to the new time.** Dinner, bath and bed at the new clock time on Sunday help everyone adjust before school.
- **Get Monday ready.** Lay out clothes and pack bags on Sunday evening, so Monday morning feels normal.

## FAQ

### How can I keep Halloween calm for young kids?

Plan the whole weekend, not just the evening. Keep Saturday morning slow, go out early for a short route, and stop while it is still fun. Use Sunday to rest before school.

### What should kids wear for trick-or-treating on a warm night?

Light layers under the costume, comfortable shoes, and face paint or a hat instead of a full mask. Pack a thin sweater for when it cools down, plus water.

### How do I make my child easy to see after dark?

Add reflective tape to the costume or treat bag, and give your child a small flashlight or glow stick. Stay together, walk on sidewalks and cross at corners.

### What if my child finds Halloween overwhelming?

Keep it short, go early, preview the route in daylight, and agree on a signal for "I'm done." Handing out treats at home is a good option too.
