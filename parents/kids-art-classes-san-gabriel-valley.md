> **Key takeaways**
> - Drawing, painting, clay and mixed media classes feel very different. Start with what your child already loves to make.
> - Little kids need short, playful sessions. Older kids are ready for real skills and longer projects.
> - Ask how big the group is, what materials are used, and what kids take home. Then try one class.

From Pasadena and Alhambra to Arcadia and Monrovia, the San Gabriel Valley has art classes in studios, community rooms and after-school programs. That is good news, but it can make choosing hard. Here is how the main kinds of art class differ, what fits at each age, what to ask, and how to tell after one visit whether a class is right.

## The main kinds of kids' art class

- **Drawing.** Pencils, markers, charcoal and pastels. Kids learn to look closely, sketch shapes and add shading. Quiet and focused, and easy to keep going at home.
- **Painting.** Watercolor, tempera or acrylic. More color, more mess, and lots of room to experiment. Good for kids who love bold, bright work.
- **Clay.** Pinch pots, coil pots, small animals and tiles. Very hands-on. Some classes use air-dry clay. Others fire pieces in a kiln, so finished work may come home a week or two later.
- **Mixed media.** Collage, paint, paper, fabric and found objects in one project. Great for kids who like to try a bit of everything.

Many classes mix two or more of these across a term. If your child can't decide, a mixed media class is a gentle place to start.

## What fits at each age

### Ages 3 to 5: messy, short and playful

At this age, art is about the doing, not the result. Look for short sessions, big brushes and chunky crayons, and lots of freedom. A teacher who says "tell me about your picture" instead of "what is it?" is a good sign. Expect paint on sleeves. Send old clothes.

A parent-and-child class can help a shy child settle in. Finger painting, play dough and simple collage all build the hand muscles kids later use to write.

### Ages 6 to 9: learning real skills

Kids this age can follow steps and start to notice their own progress. A good class teaches simple skills, like mixing colors, drawing from a real object, or rolling an even coil of clay, and still leaves room for each child's own ideas.

Watch for projects where every child's work looks a little different. If all the paintings on the wall look the same, the class may be more about copying than creating.

### Ages 10 to 13: going deeper

Older kids often want to get good at one thing. They may ask for drawing from life, painting landscapes, or bigger clay pieces. Look for longer sessions, some choice in projects, and a teacher who gives honest, kind feedback. Some kids at this age also enjoy keeping a sketchbook between classes.

## Questions to ask before you sign up

1. **How many kids are in a class?** Smaller groups mean more help when a child gets stuck.
2. **What materials do you use?** Ask whether the materials are made for kids and labelled non-toxic. Ask if anything needs to come from home.
3. **What will my child take home?** Some classes send work home each week. Clay classes may hold pieces until they are dry or fired.
4. **How do you handle different skill levels?** A mixed group works well when the teacher adjusts each project.
5. **What should my child wear?** Most art rooms say old clothes or a smock. It saves a lot of laundry stress.

## How to judge a first class

You can learn a lot from one visit. Afterwards, think about:

- **How did your child walk out?** Chatty and proud is a great sign, even if the project isn't finished.
- **Did the teacher learn their name?** Small things like this show a child was seen.
- **Was there time to make, not just listen?** Kids should spend most of the class with materials in their hands.
- **Was the room calm and safe?** Tools put away, spills cleaned up, and kids not left waiting long.
- **Did your child want to tell you about their work?** That spark is what you are looking for.

One so-so class is not always the end. Kids can be tired or shy the first time. If it still doesn't click after a fair try, it is fine to look again. ## Fitting art into a San Gabriel Valley week

- **Try the drive first.** The 210 and the 10 can be slow after school. A class 10 minutes away at noon may take much longer at 4 pm.
- **Pick the right time of day.** Some kids focus best right after school. Others need a snack and a break first.
- **Think about the heat.** Afternoons in the valley can stay hot into fall. An indoor art class is a good choice on very warm days.
- **Keep it simple.** One art class a week is plenty for most kids alongside school.

## FAQ

### What age can kids start art classes?

Many art classes welcome children from about age three, often with short, playful sessions. Check the age range a class gives, and ask the teacher if you are unsure.

### Is clay or painting better for young kids?

Both can work. Clay suits kids who love to squish and build. Painting suits kids who love color. Try one and watch how your child responds.

### What should my child wear to an art class?

Old clothes or a smock, and shoes you don't mind getting a little messy. Ask whether the class has aprons.
