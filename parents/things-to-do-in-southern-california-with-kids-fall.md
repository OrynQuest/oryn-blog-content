> **Key takeaways**
> - Fall here can be warm, windy, cool and wet in the same month, so plan by the kind of day.
> - Cool mornings are the time for outdoor outings. Keep an indoor backup for hot, windy days.
> - After the clocks go back in early November, move outdoor time earlier and enjoy the early dark.
> - One regular class gives the season a steady rhythm, whatever the weather does.

Fall in Southern California rarely looks like the postcard version. Most leaves stay on the trees, a warm spell can arrive in late October, and the first real rain may come late. From mid-October to early December, the weather can change a lot from week to week.

So plan by the kind of day you get. Below are ideas for cool mornings, Santa Ana wind and heat days, short evenings after the clock change, and rainy days, then harvest and holiday outings and one regular class. For low-cost weekend ideas that work all year, see our [weekend activities guide](/blog/weekend-activities-for-kids-on-any-budget-2026-parent-s).

## Cooler mornings: get outside early

Fall mornings are often the most comfortable part of the day, especially near the coast. Afternoons can still turn warm, and inland valleys can stay hot well into the season. So do outdoor things before lunch.

Good cool-morning outings:

- **A short trail walk.** Pick an easy, well-used path and let your child set the pace. Fall is a good time to look for acorns, seed pods and the trees that do change color.
- **The beach after summer.** Many beaches feel calmer once school is back in session. Walk the sand, build something, look for shells and birds at the water's edge.
- **A bike or scooter ride.** Cooler air makes a longer loop more fun for small legs.
- **A farmers market walk.** Let each child choose one fall fruit or vegetable to cook together later.

Bring layers, water and sunscreen; the sun is still strong in fall. Check that a trail or park is open before you go, since closures can change after storms or fires.

## Santa Ana wind and heat days: have an indoor plan

Santa Ana winds are the warm, dry, gusty winds that blow from the inland deserts toward the coast, most often in fall and winter. They can push temperatures up fast, dry the air out and raise fire danger. On some days, smoke or dust lowers the air quality too.

On those days, keep the plan indoors and simple:

- **The library.** Cool, quiet and full of books.
- **An indoor class or drop-in.** Art, music, dance, cooking or a STEM session all work the same on a windy day.
- **A kitchen project.** Bake something easy, or make lunch together with a child in charge of one step.
- **A build day.** Blocks, cardboard boxes and tape can turn into a fort, a city or a racetrack.

Check the local air quality when the wind picks up or smoke is around, and save the park for a calmer day.

## After the clock change: plans for shorter evenings

The clocks go back an hour in early November, and it gets dark around dinnertime. After-school park time shrinks.

A few ways to adjust:

- **Go outside right after school.** Even a short walk or play in the yard before dark helps.
- **Move bigger outdoor plans to the weekend,** when you have the daylight.
- **Enjoy the early dark.** Step outside after dinner and look at the moon and the brightest stars before bedtime.
- **Make evenings cozy.** Cook dinner together, read aloud, or start a family project that runs all season.
- **Try a late-afternoon indoor class,** so the end of the day has something to look forward to.

## Rainy days: keep a short list ready

Rain is less common here than in many places, so it can catch families off guard. Roads get slower, and plans with a long drive can fall apart. Keep a short list on the fridge:

- **A puddle walk** in rain boots on a gentle day, close to home. Stay away from flowing water in creeks, washes and storm channels.
- **An indoor obstacle course** made from cushions, chairs and a blanket tunnel.
- **A craft box** with paper, glue, tape and whatever is left over from past projects.
- **Kitchen science,** like testing which things float and sink in a bowl of water.
- **An online class** from home, or an indoor class close by.

## Harvest and holiday-season outings

In October, many communities have pumpkin patches, corn mazes and farm stands, and some mountain areas are known for apple picking. These are often busiest on weekends and just before Halloween, so a weekday afternoon or an early weekend morning can be calmer.

After Halloween comes the holiday season. Tree lots open, neighborhoods put up light displays, and many towns hold holiday markets. Because it gets dark earlier, young kids can see the lights before bedtime.

Check an event's own page for hours and tickets, bring a warm layer for chilly evenings, and pick one outing a weekend rather than several.

## Add one regular class for the season

Weather plans change week to week. A regular class does not. One weekly activity gives the season a steady rhythm, and an indoor class works the same in a Santa Ana wind or a rainstorm.

Cooking and baking fit the holiday season, and arts and crafts can turn into homemade gifts. Start with what your child already loves, and keep the drive short so the class still works once it gets dark early. ## FAQ

### What are good things to do in Southern California with kids in the fall?

Plan by the weather. Go outside on cool mornings, keep indoor ideas ready for hot, windy or rainy days, and add a pumpkin patch or a holiday light display. One regular class anchors the week.

### What can kids do indoors on a Santa Ana wind day?

Try the library, a kitchen project, a build day with boxes and tape, or an indoor class or drop-in. Check the local air quality before any outdoor play.

### How do we keep outdoor time once it gets dark early?

Go outside right after school, even for a short walk, and move bigger outdoor plans to the weekend. Use the early dark for stargazing before bedtime.

### What should we do with kids on a rainy day in Southern California?

Keep a short list ready: an indoor obstacle course, a craft box, kitchen science or an online class. If you go out in the rain, stay away from creeks, washes and storm channels.
