> **Key takeaways**
> - Readiness, not age, decides the start.
> - Ages 1–3: parent-and-child classes build comfort, not strokes.
> - Ages 4–6: most children can begin structured lessons.

## How do you know a child is ready?

A child who will put their face in the bath water, follow a two-step instruction and stay in a group for twenty minutes is usually ready for a beginner class.

## What does a first swim lesson look like?

Small groups, a warm pool, and a coach in the water. Expect floating, kicking on a noodle and blowing bubbles before any stroke work.

## FAQ

### Is 3 too young for swim lessons?
Not for a parent-and-child class. For a drop-off beginner class, most coaches suggest waiting until 4.

### How many lessons a week?
One or two. Consistency over months matters more than intensity in one week.
